# The 10 Safety Rules (Read This Before Anything Else)

Every buy proposal must pass **10 safety gates**. The *first* failure stops the trade — no appeals. These rules are the real product here; the strategy finds opportunities, but these rules keep you alive. All numbers below use a **₹10,00,000 example account** so you can feel them.

## The gates, in order

1. **Daily loss halt (–3% = –₹30,000).**
   Already lost ₹30,000 today? Done. No more trades. Beginners blow up by "revenge trading" after losses — this rule removes your ability to do that. Tomorrow is a new day.

2. **All-time circuit breaker (–10% from your peak).**
   If your account ever falls 10% below the highest it has *ever* been (₹10L peak → ₹9L), everything halts until you personally intervene. This is the "stop the bleeding and rethink" rule.

3. **Max 8 open trades.**
   Prevents turning your account into an unmanageable zoo of positions you can't follow.

4. **One trade per stock.**
   Already own Reliance? No second helping, no matter how good it looks. Concentration kills.

5. **Cash reserve (₹2,00,000 untouched).**
   Trading stops if free cash falls below ₹2L. Beginners go all-in; professionals always keep dry powder.

6. **Minimum 1:2 reward-to-risk.**
   Risking ₹2,000 on a trade that can only make ₹2,500? Rejected. The math of survival requires wins to outweigh losses — with a 40% win rate you still profit *only if* average wins are 2× average losses.

7. **Not too hot, not too cold (volatility 0.3–4%).**
   Stocks that barely move waste your day; stocks that swing wildly can gap past your stop-loss (you lose *more* than planned). Both are rejected.

8. **Portfolio heat cap (6% = ₹60,000).**
   Add up the worst case of *every* open trade (entry minus stop-loss, times quantity). If that total exceeds ₹60,000, no new trades. Why it matters: 8 trades × 2% risk each = 16% danger if the whole market drops together. The heat cap forces the *combined* danger to stay small.

9. **Smart position sizing (the interesting one).**
   Start with 2% risk per trade (₹20,000 on ₹10L), then adjust three ways:
   - **Calm stock? Bigger size. Wild stock? Smaller.** A ₹2 stop on a sleepy stock and a ₹2 stop on a crazy stock are not the same risk — size is scaled so each trade risks equally in *volatility* terms.
   - **Bad day? Half size.** Once the day's loss passes half the daily limit, every new trade is automatically halved. Throttle before halt.
   - **Weak signal? Smaller.** A shaky 0.3-confidence setup gets a smaller position than a 0.9 one.

10. **Can you actually afford it?**
    Final sanity check against real cash. If not, size shrinks to what cash allows — or the trade dies.

## A worked example (₹10,00,000 account)

Signal: buy XYZ at ₹500, stop-loss ₹490 (risk ₹10/share), target ₹520, confidence 0.8, volatility normal.

- Base risk money: 2% × ₹10L = ₹20,000 → 2,000 shares by risk math
- But max 8% per stock = ₹80,000 → max 160 shares → **160 shares**
- Confidence scaling: 160 × (0.5 + 0.5×0.8) = 160 × 0.9 = **144 shares**
- Cost check: 144 × ₹500 = ₹72,000 < cash ✓
- Verdict: **APPROVED, 144 shares. Worst case: 144 × ₹10 = ₹1,440 (~0.14% of account).**

That last line is the whole philosophy: *no single trade can hurt you*. Losses become tuition fees, not tragedies.

## Beginner takeaways

- Professionals obsess over position size; beginners obsess over stock tips. This file is the most important one in the project.
- If you change one thing while learning, change the *daily loss limit* down (e.g., 3% → 1%) — trade smaller while you're paying tuition.
- Every rejection is logged with its reason — your report shows which rule saves you most often. That's a mirror of your strategy's weaknesses.
